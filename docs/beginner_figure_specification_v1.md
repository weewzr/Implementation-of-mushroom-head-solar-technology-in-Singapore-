# Beginner-First Figure Specification

These figures belong before the formal mathematics. Their purpose is physical understanding, not decoration.

## B1 — From sunlight to usable electricity
Sun -> rays -> PV module -> DC electricity -> inverter/load/grid. Show heat/reflection losses. No equations. Message: PV converts part of intercepted solar radiation; it does not create energy.

## B2 — Three ways sunlight reaches PV
One panel with direct beam from the Sun, diffuse rays from the sky/clouds, and reflected rays from the ground. Message: 3-D geometry changes access to each pathway differently.

## B3 — Why angle matters
The same panel in three orientations under parallel rays: facing beam, tilted, nearly edge-on. Show projected silhouette. No equations. Message: physical area is unchanged, but area presented toward the beam changes.

## B4 — Why more panels can become worse
Sparse and dense 3-D canopies side by side. Show open rays in the sparse case and blocked rays/shadows in the dense case. Message: packing can increase area while reducing productivity through self-shading and lost sky view.

## B5 — Why the mushroom idea exists
Equal horizontal land boxes: flat panel versus mushroom canopy on a stem. Show additional active surface above the same footprint and open/usable space below. Do not imply superiority.

## B6 — What rotation is trying to accomplish
Morning/noon/evening Sun positions, canopy rotation arrow, plus motor-energy, wind-load and wear/friction arrows. Message: tracking is useful only if additional value exceeds mechanical penalties.

## B7 — Fair geometry comparison
Flat, paraboloid, hemisphere, folded, petals and faceted/free-form candidates inside identical resource boxes. Labels: same land; same PV-area budget; same height envelope; same weather; same loss model.

## B8 — From picture to mathematics
Physical picture -> coordinates -> surface normal -> Sun direction -> illuminated/shaded facet -> surface integration -> analytical branch / discrete branch -> verification -> validation.

## Figure-quality rules
1. One physical idea per figure.
2. Large labels; minimal text.
3. No unexplained mathematical symbols in B1-B7.
4. B8 may introduce symbol names visually but must not derive equations.
5. Use canonical ENU orientation whenever axes are shown.
6. Direct, diffuse and reflected rays must remain visually distinguishable.
7. Every caption states what the figure teaches and why it matters.
8. Every figure is discussed in adjacent report text.
9. Mark conceptual/not-to-scale figures where appropriate.
10. Never imply that the mushroom or another candidate is already superior.
