# Beginner-First Figure Specification

These figures sit before the main mathematics. Their purpose is conceptual understanding, not numerical evidence. Each should be understandable without reading an equation.

## Figure B1 - From sunlight to useful electricity
Show, left to right: Sun -> light rays -> PV module -> DC electricity -> inverter/load/grid. Use arrows for energy flow. Include a small loss branch labelled heat/losses. No equations in the figure.

**Teaching message:** a PV panel converts part of received sunlight into useful electricity; geometry first changes how much light reaches the panel.

## Figure B2 - Three ways light can reach a 3-D panel
Show one mushroom canopy under a sky. Use three visually distinct ray paths:
1. direct ray from Sun;
2. diffuse rays from several sky directions/clouds;
3. reflected ray from ground.
Include a simple shadow region under one part of the canopy.

**Teaching message:** a surface can receive light even when it is not pointed directly at the Sun, but obstruction can remove both direct and sky access.

## Figure B3 - Why angle matters
Three equal rectangular panels: one facing the ray, one tilted, one facing away. Beside them show large, smaller and zero front-side direct capture using simple filled projected silhouettes.

**Teaching message:** the same physical panel presents a different effective receiving area depending on orientation.

## Figure B4 - Why more panel area can still lose
Two equal land footprints. Left: simple flat panel, almost all exposed. Right: densely packed 3-D panels with some surfaces shaded. Use a balance graphic: “more active area” versus “lower average light per area.”

**Teaching message:** land packing and per-area productivity compete.

## Figure B5 - What rotation is trying to do
Show morning/noon/evening Sun positions around a mushroom. Use three ghosted cap orientations or a rotation arrow. Add a small motor/bearing icon and wind arrow.

**Teaching message:** tracking may improve orientation but introduces motor energy, wind load, wear and mechanical complexity.

## Figure B6 - Fair geometry comparison
Show flat, paraboloid mushroom, hemisphere, folded roof, petals and faceted canopy inside identical dashed resource boxes labelled same land, same PV-area budget, same maximum height, same weather.

**Teaching message:** geometry is the variable; resources and environmental inputs must be held consistent.

## Figure B7 - From picture to mathematics
A staircase/bridge:
physical picture -> identify surfaces/directions -> define coordinates -> state assumptions -> write governing relation -> analytical or discrete solution -> verify -> validate -> compare designs.

**Teaching message:** equations are abstractions of an already-understood physical system, not the starting point.

## Figure rules
- no decorative stock imagery;
- minimal text;
- no unexplained symbols;
- child-readable main message;
- technical caption below each figure;
- colour/shape coding must remain consistent between figures;
- every figure must be discussed immediately in the report;
- equations belong in the following mathematical section, not inside these beginner figures.
