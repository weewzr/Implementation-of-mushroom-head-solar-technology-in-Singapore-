\begin{abstract}
Singapore's limited land motivates photovoltaic designs that must be judged by useful solar resource per constrained footprint rather than installed module area alone. This project began from a rotating mushroom-head photovoltaic hypothesis and asks, without assuming that geometry wins, whether three-dimensional surfaces can improve land-normalised annual solar collection under fair resource accounting. Analytical geometry and limiting cases are used to verify a Rust triangular-mesh model combining solar position, incidence, explicit visibility/self-shadowing, sky-view treatment and annual integration with NASA POWER Singapore 2024 development weather. A retained, audited DEVELOPMENT\_NOT\_SERIS comparison now evaluates flat, paraboloidal mushroom, hemisphere, faceted-canopy and folded-surface candidates under equal-land and equal-PV contracts, with explicit packing efficiency and land-energy accounting and converged mesh/sky settings. The work also establishes a narrow rigid-origami kinematic foundation and is extending it toward controlled deployable fixtures. The present validated scope is irradiance geometry rather than complete electricity, structural, cost or carbon performance; those layers are explicitly reserved for subsequent model stages. The intended contribution is a reproducible framework that separates geometric packing from per-area solar productivity so later engineering and techno-economic conclusions can be tested rather than assumed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and research question}
Singapore's solar-energy problem is not simply how to install more photovoltaic (PV) module area. It is how to obtain useful electrical energy from surfaces that compete for scarce urban space. The Energy Market Authority (EMA) identifies solar as Singapore's most viable domestic renewable resource, while also noting that limited land constrains deployment and that rooftops, water surfaces and other usable spaces must be exploited carefully \cite{ema_solar_2026}. In 2026 Singapore raised its deployment target to \SI{3}{\giga\watt}p by 2030 after reaching \SI{2}{\giga\watt}p in 2025; EMA explicitly links further deployment to viable rooftops, land, water and innovative canopy-like surfaces \cite{ema_target_2026}.

This motivates a geometric question. A three-dimensional canopy can place more active PV area above a given horizontal footprint than a single flat surface, but extra area is not automatically useful. Curvature and folding change incidence angle, sky view and self-shading; additional structure may also introduce thermal, electrical and mechanical penalties. A fair comparison must therefore separate \emph{how much PV area fits} from \emph{how productive that area remains}.

The project originated from a rotating mushroom-head PV concept: a raised, curved collector intended to use a constrained footprint more intensively. That idea is retained as the research origin, not as a presumed optimum. The central falsifiable research question is:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Under equal and explicitly declared resource constraints, can a three-dimensional PV geometry increase useful solar-energy collection per unit land footprint in Singapore, and do any gains remain after thermal, electrical and engineering penalties are introduced?}
\end{quote}

Phase I answers the irradiance-geometry part of this question for a controlled set of fixed candidates. Phase II converts the audited irradiance evidence into thermal and electrical quantities. Structural, manufacturing, cost and carbon layers are treated separately so that a favourable irradiance result cannot by itself be mistaken for a complete engineering recommendation.

\section{Beginner-first visual explanation}
The physical story is simple before it becomes mathematical. Sunlight reaches a PV surface; the surface receives direct light from the Sun, diffuse light from the visible sky and reflected light from the surroundings. The PV module converts part of the received radiation to DC electricity, and an inverter converts DC to AC. Temperature and system losses reduce the useful output.

The geometry question can be pictured as two separate tests. In an \emph{equal-land} comparison, every candidate receives the same horizontal footprint and may contain different active PV area. In an \emph{equal-PV} comparison, every candidate receives the same active PV area and may occupy a different footprint.

\begin{equation}

\label{eq:beginner-energy-chain}
\end{equation}
This is a conceptual accounting relation rather than the numerical model. The following sections replace each term with explicit geometry, irradiance, temperature and electrical equations.

\section{Singapore context and design motivation}
Singapore's solar resource is substantial: EMA reports average annual solar irradiance of approximately \SI{1580}{\kilo\watt\hour\per\metre\squared\per\year} \cite{ema_solar_2026}. The deployment constraint is spatial rather than a lack of sunlight. EMA notes that limited land requires trade-offs between solar installations and competing uses, and current policy therefore emphasises deployment on rooftops, reservoirs, land and other viable surfaces \cite{ema_solar_2026,ema_target_2026}.

This context changes the appropriate engineering metric. Maximising annual energy per square metre of PV material is useful for module productivity, but it does not by itself answer a land-constrained design question. Conversely, maximising active PV area above a footprint can be misleading if much of that additional surface is poorly illuminated. This study therefore reports both active-area-normalised and land-area-normalised quantities.

For a candidate with active PV area $A_{\rm PV}$ and projected land footprint $A_{\rm land}$, the packing ratio is
\begin{equation}
\Pi=\frac{A_{\rm PV}}{A_{\rm land}}.
\label{eq:packing-ratio-intro}
\end{equation}
Packing ratio measures geometric area intensification only; it says nothing about how well the added PV area is illuminated. Later sections therefore pair $\Pi$ with an irradiance-productivity measure and show how the two combine into the land-energy multiplier. This separation is central to the paper's fair-comparison framework.

The development weather used in the present numerical evidence is NASA POWER Singapore 2024 and remains labelled \texttt{DEVELOPMENT\_NOT\_SERIS}. The label is a provenance limitation, not a statement that the underlying physics is provisional. Final promotion requires the declared evidence gates and, where appropriate, higher-authority Singapore validation data.

\section{Original mushroom-head concept and controlled candidate family}
The founding concept is a raised PV canopy whose head resembles a shallow paraboloid or mushroom cap. Its intended advantage is geometric: curvature can place more active PV area above a constrained footprint and distribute surface orientations across the sky. Its possible disadvantages are equally important: parts of the surface can face away from the Sun, shade one another, see less diffuse sky, run at different temperatures, or require additional structure and actuation.

\centering

The controlled fixed-geometry study therefore includes a flat reference, the frozen paraboloidal mushroom candidate, a hemisphere, a faceted canopy and a folded surface. These candidates are not a ranking of commercial designs. They are deliberately different geometric tests of the same resource-accounting question. The mushroom concept is the origin of the investigation; the flat surface remains the reference against which packing and irradiance productivity are interpreted.

Rotation and deployability are kept outside this fixed comparison. They introduce actuator energy, kinematic constraints, stowage requirements and reliability questions that cannot be credited for free. The later origami and tracking sections therefore use separate model layers and validation gates.

The transition from concept to mathematics begins with a common coordinate system. Once the Sun direction $\mathbf{s}$ and each surface normal $\mathbf{n}$ are defined consistently, the incidence angle follows from their dot product; visibility, sky view and resource normalisation can then be added without changing the physical question.

\section{Coordinate and sign conventions}
The canonical local frame is right-handed East--North--Up (ENU): \(+x\) east, \(+y\) north and \(+z\) vertically upward. A position vector is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r=(x,y,z).
\label{eq:position-enu}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathbf r\) is position (\si{\metre}); \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\) are the east, north and upward coordinates respectively (\si{\metre}). These axes are project definitions rather than empirical parameters.

Solar and facet azimuth are measured clockwise from geographic north: north \(=0^\circ\), east \(=90^\circ\), south \(=180^\circ\), west \(=270^\circ\). The canonical Rust implementation uses radians internally. For solar elevation \(\alpha\) and solar azimuth \(\gamma_s\), the ENU unit vector toward the Sun is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf s=
\left(
\cos\alpha\sin\gamma_s,\,
\cos\alpha\cos\gamma_s,\,
\sin\alpha
\right).
\label{eq:sun-vector-enu}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathbf s\) is the dimensionless unit vector from the receiving point toward the Sun; \(\alpha\) is solar elevation above the local horizon (rad in Rust); and \(\gamma_s\) is solar azimuth clockwise from north (rad in Rust). At zenith Eq.~\eqref{eq:sun-vector-enu} gives \((0,0,1)\); at the eastern horizon it gives \((1,0,0)\); at the northern horizon it gives \((0,1,0)\). Its norm is unity.

For facet tilt \(\beta\) from horizontal and facet azimuth \(\gamma_p\),
\begin{equation}
\mathbf n=
\left(
\sin\beta\sin\gamma_p,\,
\sin\beta\cos\gamma_p,\,
\cos\beta
\right).
\label{eq:facet-normal-enu}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathbf n\) is the outward/front-side unit normal (dimensionless); \(\beta\) is facet tilt from horizontal (rad in Rust); and \(\gamma_p\) is facet azimuth clockwise from north (rad in Rust).

\centering
\includesvg[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/enu_coordinate_convention}

\paragraph{Physical Interpretation.}
The ENU convention makes the vertical component directly represent upward projection. An upward horizontal module has \(\mathbf n=(0,0,1)\).

\paragraph{Engineering Implication.}
Geometry generators, Sun vectors, facet normals, visibility rays and future mechanical axes must use this convention. External datasets or libraries using different azimuth definitions must be converted at the data boundary.

\section{Solar-position equations}
For preliminary transparent calculations, the Cooper (1969) declination approximation is
\begin{equation}
\delta(n)=23.45^{\circ}\sin\!\left[\frac{360^{\circ}}{365}(284+n)\right].
\label{eq:cooper}
\end{equation}
Here $\delta$ is solar declination (degrees); $n$ is ordinal day of a non-leap year (dimensionless day index); $23.45^{\circ}$ is the approximate declination amplitude in the Cooper engineering correlation; $360^{\circ}$ is one complete angular cycle; $365$ is the non-leap-year period assumed by the approximation; and $284$ is its phase-offset constant. Equation~\eqref{eq:cooper} is an astronomical approximation, not an exact identity or a project-fitted model. Validated simulations should use a traceable higher-accuracy solar-position method.

Using local apparent solar time,
\begin{equation}
H=15^{\circ}\,\si{\per\hour}\left(t_{\mathrm{solar}}-12\,\si{\hour}\right).
\label{eq:hour-angle}
\end{equation}
Here $H$ is solar hour angle (degrees); $t_{\mathrm{solar}}$ is local apparent solar time (hours); $15^{\circ}\,\si{\per\hour}$ follows from $360^{\circ}/24\,\si{\hour}$; and $12\,\si{\hour}$ denotes apparent solar noon, where $H=0^{\circ}$. Civil clock time must not be substituted directly without longitude and equation-of-time correction.

Solar elevation satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sin\alpha=\sin\phi\sin\delta+\cos\phi\cos\delta\cos H.
\label{eq:elevation}
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha$ is solar elevation above the local horizon; $\phi$ is geographic latitude; $\delta$ is solar declination; and $H$ is solar hour angle. All angular quantities must use a consistent convention; the numerical implementation converts angles to radians before evaluating trigonometric functions.

\section{Direct and diffuse incidence}
For an infinitesimal PV element,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm dP_{\mathrm{dir}}=\eta(T)DNI(t)V(t)[\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf s(t)]_{+}\,\mathrm dA.
\label{eq:direct}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathrm dP_{\mathrm{dir}}$ is incremental direct-derived electrical power (W); $\eta(T)$ is conversion efficiency (dimensionless); $DNI$ is direct normal irradiance (\si{\watt\per\square\metre}); $V$ is direct visibility (dimensionless); $\mathbf n$ and $\mathbf s$ are unit vectors (dimensionless); $[x]_+=\max(0,x)$ is the positive-part operator; and $\mathrm dA$ is active PV area (\si{\square\metre}). The unit check is $(\si{\watt\per\square\metre})(\si{\square\metre})=\si{\watt}$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includesvg[width=0.90\textwidth]{../figures/direct_incidence_geometry}
\caption{Direct-beam incidence convention for a PV facet. The front-side contribution is proportional to $[\mathbf n\!\cdot\!\mathbf s]_+$ and visibility $V$; grazing and backside incidence contribute zero to the present monofacial direct term.}
\label{fig:direct-incidence}
\end{figure}

The first diffuse approximation is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm dP_{\mathrm{diff}}=\eta(T)DHI(t)\frac{1+\cos\beta}{2}\,\mathrm dA.
\label{eq:diffuse}
\end{equation}
Here $DHI$ is diffuse horizontal irradiance (\si{\watt\per\square\metre}); $\beta$ is surface tilt from horizontal; and $(1+\cos\beta)/2$ is the unobstructed isotropic-sky view factor. The factor $1/2$ follows from ideal hemispherical geometry and is not an empirical Singapore coefficient.

\section{Paraboloidal mushroom}
The founding analytical geometry is
\begin{equation}
z(r)=h\left(1-\frac{r^2}{R^2}\right),\qquad k=\frac{h}{R}.
\label{eq:surface}
\end{equation}
Here $z$ is surface height (m); $r$ is radial coordinate (m); $h$ is apex height (m); $R$ is footprint radius (m); and $k$ is dimensionless curvature/aspect ratio.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includesvg[width=0.90\textwidth]{../figures/paraboloid_geometry}
\caption{Labelled cross-section of the founding analytical paraboloidal mushroom cap. The diagram identifies footprint radius $R$, apex height $h$, radial coordinate $r$, surface height $z(r)$ and a representative local outward normal. Geometry only---not a validated optimum or performance result.}
\label{fig:mushroom-geometry}
\end{figure}

Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:surface},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm dz}{\mathrm dr}=-\frac{2hr}{R^2}.
\end{equation}
The slope is dimensionless. The numerical factor 2 arises exactly from differentiating $r^2$.

The axisymmetric area is
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{PV}}=2\pi\int_0^Rr\sqrt{1+\frac{4h^2r^2}{R^4}}\,\mathrm dr.
\end{equation}
Here $A_{\mathrm{PV}}$ is active curved area (\si{\square\metre}); $2\pi r\,\mathrm dr$ is the annular area contribution; and the square-root term is the dimensionless slope correction.

Exact integration gives
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{PV}}=\frac{\pi R^2}{6k^2}\left[(1+4k^2)^{3/2}-1\right].
\end{equation}
The factors 4 and 6 arise from the derivative and exact integral, not empirical fitting.

With $A_{\mathrm{foot}}=\pi R^2$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Pi=\frac{A_{\mathrm{PV}}}{A_{\mathrm{foot}}}=\frac{(1+4k^2)^{3/2}-1}{6k^2}}.
\label{eq:packing-paraboloid}
\end{equation}
Here $\Pi$ is the dimensionless PV packing ratio and $A_{\mathrm{foot}}$ is horizontal footprint area (\si{\square\metre}). The limiting case $k\to0$ gives $\Pi\to1$, recovering a flat disk.

\section{Diffuse-light analytical limit}
Under the unobstructed isotropic-sky benchmark, the diffuse-derived electrical power over surface \(S\) is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{diff}}
=
\eta DHI
\int_S
\frac{1+\cos\beta}{2}\,\mathrm dA.
\label{eq:diffuse-integrated}
\end{equation}
Here \(P_{\mathrm{diff}}\) is diffuse-derived electrical power (W); \(\eta\) is photovoltaic conversion efficiency (dimensionless); \(DHI\) is diffuse horizontal irradiance (\si{\watt\per\square\metre}); \(\beta\) is local surface tilt from horizontal; \(S\) is the receiving PV surface; and \(\mathrm dA\) is differential active area (\si{\square\metre}). The factor \(1/2\) is the ideal hemispherical isotropic-sky coefficient, not a Singapore empirical parameter.

Separating the integral gives
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{diff}}
=
\frac{\eta DHI}{2}
\left(
\int_S\mathrm dA
+
\int_S\cos\beta\,\mathrm dA
\right).
\end{equation}
For a single-valued upward-facing graph surface with no overhangs, its vertical projection satisfies
\begin{equation}
\int_S\cos\beta\,\mathrm dA=A_{\mathrm{foot}}.
\label{eq:vertical-projection}
\end{equation}
Here \(A_{\mathrm{foot}}\) is the horizontal projected footprint (\si{\square\metre}). Since \(\int_S\mathrm dA=A_{\mathrm{PV}}\), the benchmark becomes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
P_{\mathrm{diff}}
=
\frac{\eta DHI}{2}
\left(
A_{\mathrm{PV}}+A_{\mathrm{foot}}
\right)
}.
\label{eq:diffuse-cap-limit}
\end{equation}
The unit check is
\((\si{\watt\per\square\metre})(\si{\square\metre})=\si{\watt}\).

\paragraph{Physical Interpretation.}
For this idealised convex/no-occlusion benchmark, additional upward-facing surface area can intercept additional isotropic diffuse sky radiation while the projected-area term remains fixed by the footprint. This does not imply that arbitrarily increasing curvature produces a real annual-yield gain because real facets obscure portions of the sky and may obstruct one another.

\paragraph{Engineering Implication.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:diffuse-cap-limit} is an analytical benchmark for later Rust and ray/sky-view implementations. It must not be used as a validated Singapore annual-yield model. Final 3-D calculations require time-correlated DHI, an anisotropic sky treatment and explicit sky visibility.

\section{Land-energy multiplier}
For the present irradiance-level controlled comparison, define the land-energy multiplier.
\begin{equation}
M_L=\frac{H_{\mathrm{POA},3D}/A_{\mathrm{land}}}{H_{\mathrm{POA,base}}/A_{\mathrm{land}}}=\frac{H_{\mathrm{POA},3D}}{H_{\mathrm{POA,base}}}.
\label{eq:land-multiplication-definition}
\end{equation}
Here \(M_L\) is dimensionless; \(H_{\mathrm{POA},3D}\) and \(H_{\mathrm{POA,base}}\) are annual incident POA energies for the candidate and flat reference under the same land contract. At this stage \(M_L\) is an irradiance-level metric, not annual electrical energy.

Define the packing ratio
\begin{equation}
\Pi=\frac{A_{\mathrm{PV}}}{A_{\mathrm{land}}}.
\label{eq:packing-land}
\end{equation}
Here \(\Pi\) is dimensionless; \(A_{\mathrm{PV}}\) is active photovoltaic area (\si{\square\metre}); and \(A_{\mathrm{land}}\) is constrained horizontal site area (\si{\square\metre}).

Multiplying and dividing Eq.~\eqref{eq:land-multiplication-definition} by \(\Pi\) gives
\begin{equation}
M_L=\Pi\frac{H_{\mathrm{POA},3D}/A_{\mathrm{PV},3D}}{H_{\mathrm{POA,base}}/A_{\mathrm{PV,base}}}.
\end{equation}
All terms are dimensionless because the energy ratio and \(\Pi\) are dimensionless. Define packed-PV productivity
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}=\frac{H_{\mathrm{POA},3D}/A_{\mathrm{PV},3D}}{H_{\mathrm{POA,base}}/A_{\mathrm{PV,base}}}.
\label{eq:packing-productivity}
\end{equation}
Here \(\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}\) is average packed-PV productivity relative to the baseline (dimensionless). Therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{M_L=\Pi\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}}.
\label{eq:land-product}
\end{equation}
Here \(M_L\), \(\Pi\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}\) are dimensionless.

For a differentiable relationship \(\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}(\Pi)\),
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm dM_L}{\mathrm d\Pi}
=
\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}
+
\Pi\frac{\mathrm d\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}}{\mathrm d\Pi}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathrm dM_L/\mathrm d\Pi\) and \(\mathrm d\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}/\mathrm d\Pi\) are dimensionless derivatives. An interior stationary point of the land-energy objective satisfies
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}
+
\Pi\frac{\mathrm d\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}}{\mathrm d\Pi}
=0,
\end{equation}
which can be written
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
-\frac{\mathrm d\ln\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}}{\mathrm d\ln\Pi}=1
}.
\end{equation}
The logarithmic derivative is dimensionless. The numerical value 1 follows exactly from differentiating the product in Eq.~\eqref{eq:land-product}; it is not an empirical threshold.

\paragraph{Physical Interpretation.}
\(\Pi\) measures how much active PV is packed into scarce footprint, whereas \(\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}\) measures how productive that packed PV remains. Increasing surface area alone can therefore raise \(\Pi\) while lowering \(\eta_{\mathrm{pack}}\).

\paragraph{Engineering Implication.}
A three-dimensional design is attractive for land use only when packing gain outweighs orientation, occlusion, thermal, structural, maintenance and economic penalties. The stationary condition is a diagnostic for later packing sweeps, not a validated project optimum.

\section{Sphere baseline}
A complete sphere provides a directionally symmetric analytical comparison. Its total surface area is
\begin{equation}
A_s=4\pi R^2,
\label{eq:sphere-area}
\end{equation}
where \(A_s\) is spherical surface area (\si{\square\metre}) and \(R\) is sphere radius (\si{\metre}). The factor 4 and \(\pi\) are exact geometric constants.

For parallel rays arriving from any direction, the orthogonal projected area of the sphere is the area of its circular silhouette,
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{proj}}=\pi R^2.
\label{eq:sphere-projected-area}
\end{equation}
Here \(A_{\mathrm{proj}}\) is projected area normal to the beam (\si{\square\metre}). Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\frac{A_s}{A_{\mathrm{proj}}}=4.
\end{equation}
This ratio is dimensionless and follows from exact sphere geometry; it is not an energy-yield multiplier.

\paragraph{Physical Interpretation.}
A sphere presents the same projected silhouette to a collimated beam regardless of beam direction while distributing its total surface over all outward orientations. Much of that surface is therefore oblique to, or on the back side of, any instantaneous direct beam.

\paragraph{Engineering Implication.}
The sphere is useful as a symmetry and packing benchmark, not as evidence of four-times energy collection. Any fair PV comparison must separately account for active-area budget, front/back electrical behaviour, self/structural shading, diffuse sky access and land footprint.

\section{Mechanics}
Angular momentum and rotational kinetic energy are
\begin{equation}
L=I\omega,\qquad E_k=\frac12I\omega^2.
\end{equation}
Here $L$ is angular momentum (\si{\kilogram\metre\squared\per\second}); $I$ is mass moment of inertia (\si{\kilogram\metre\squared}); $\omega$ is angular velocity (\si{\radian\per\second}); and $E_k$ is rotational kinetic energy (J). The factor $1/2$ is the exact rigid-body kinetic-energy coefficient.

A first torque balance is
\begin{equation}
\tau=I\ddot\theta+\tau_f+\tau_g+\tau_w.
\end{equation}
Here $\tau$ is actuator torque (N m); $\ddot\theta$ is angular acceleration (rad s$^{-2}$); and $\tau_f$, $\tau_g$, $\tau_w$ are frictional, gravitational and wind torques (N m).

The first drag approximation is
\begin{equation}
F_D=\frac12\rho C_DA_{\mathrm{proj}}v^2.
\end{equation}
Here $F_D$ is drag force (N); $\rho$ is air density (kg m$^{-3}$); $C_D$ is dimensionless drag coefficient; $A_{\mathrm{proj}}$ is projected area (m$^2$); and $v$ is relative wind speed (m s$^{-1}$). The $1/2$ is the standard dynamic-pressure coefficient.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includesvg[width=0.88\textwidth]{../figures/mechanical_free_body_tracking}
\caption{Conceptual mechanical free-body and tracking schematic for the founding mushroom-head candidate. The support/rotation axis, weight, representative wind resultant and moment arm, actuator torque and solar direction are shown for force/torque bookkeeping. Dimensions, wind loads, bearing reactions and actuator sizing remain unset pending sourced design inputs.}
\label{fig:mechanical-fbd}
\end{figure}


A characteristic inertial-torque scaling for a movement through angular displacement \(\Delta\theta\) over duration \(T\) is
\begin{equation}
\tau_I\sim I\frac{\Delta\theta}{T^2}.
\end{equation}
Here \(\tau_I\) is characteristic inertial torque (\si{\newton\metre}); \(I\) is mass moment of inertia (\si{\kilogram\metre\squared}); \(\Delta\theta\) is angular displacement (rad, dimensionless in SI); and \(T\) is movement duration (\si{\second}). For the same displacement executed in twice the time,
\begin{equation}
\tau_I(2T)\sim\frac{1}{4}\tau_I(T).
\end{equation}
Here the factor \(1/4\) follows exactly from the \(T^{-2}\) scaling.

\paragraph{Physical Interpretation.}
The Sun moves slowly on actuator timescales, so deliberate tracking can be quasi-static. Increasing movement duration strongly suppresses inertial torque.

\paragraph{Engineering Implication.}
The original momentum question should not become an objective to maximise angular momentum. Later actuator design should prioritise wind moment, gravitational balance, friction, locking/stow, efficiency, reliability and wear. Quantitative mechanical conclusions remain blocked until the required physical parameters in the provenance register are sourced or calculated.

\section{Tracking as an optimal-control extension}
Tracking is treated as a net-value control problem rather than as an instruction to maximise angular momentum. A generic objective is
\begin{equation}
\max_{\theta(t)}
\left\{
\int_T
\left[
P_{\mathrm{PV}}(\theta,t)
-
P_{\mathrm{motor}}(\theta,\dot\theta,\ddot\theta)
\right]\,\mathrm dt
-
C_{\mathrm{wear}}
\right\}.
\label{eq:tracking-control}
\end{equation}
Here \(\theta(t)\) is tracker orientation (rad); \(P_{\mathrm{PV}}\) is photovoltaic electrical power (W); \(P_{\mathrm{motor}}\) is actuator electrical power (W); \(T\) is the control/optimisation time horizon; and \(C_{\mathrm{wear}}\) is a wear penalty expressed in energy-equivalent units, or converted consistently if the final objective is monetary.

The time integral of power has units of energy. Therefore \(C_{\mathrm{wear}}\) must use compatible units before subtraction; a monetary wear term cannot be subtracted directly from joules or watt-hours without an explicit conversion.

\paragraph{Physical Interpretation.}
Because solar motion is slow, useful tracking may consist of infrequent or quasi-static repositioning. Additional motion is not automatically beneficial: actuator consumption, wind exposure, wear, stow events and reliability can outweigh small irradiance gains.

\paragraph{Engineering Implication.}
The eventual controller must compare fixed, scheduled-step and continuous tracking under the same geometry/resource envelope. Equation~\eqref{eq:tracking-control} is a formulation only; no optimal tracking schedule is presently claimed.

\section{Bifacial formulation and thermal-model precursor}
For a bifacial facet \(i\), define effective irradiance
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{eff},i}
=
G_{\mathrm{front},i}
+
bG_{\mathrm{rear},i}.
\label{eq:bifacial-effective}
\end{equation}
Here \(G_{\mathrm{eff},i}\), \(G_{\mathrm{front},i}\) and \(G_{\mathrm{rear},i}\) are irradiances (\si{\watt\per\square\metre}), and \(b\) is the dimensionless bifaciality factor. The equation is dimensionally consistent because both additive terms have irradiance units.

A first linear temperature correction is
\begin{equation}
\eta(T_c)
=
\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}
\left[
1+\gamma(T_c-T_{\mathrm{ref}})
\right].
\label{eq:temperature-efficiency}
\end{equation}
Here \(T_c\) is cell temperature; \(T_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is reference cell temperature on the same temperature scale; \(\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is reference conversion efficiency (dimensionless); and \(\gamma\) is the relative temperature coefficient (\si{\per\kelvin}). The product \(\gamma(T_c-T_{\mathrm{ref}})\) is dimensionless.

\paragraph{Physical Interpretation.}
A three-dimensional canopy changes both rear-surface exposure and thermal environment. Additional packed area may improve rear-sky/ground access in some geometries while dense packing may also reduce ventilation or create mutual shading.

\paragraph{Engineering Implication.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:bifacial-effective} remains a formulation only because no bifacial design basis is selected. Equation~\eqref{eq:temperature-efficiency} is the linear temperature-efficiency relation later instantiated by the selected rigid-PV electrical basis. The current NMOT thermal model and electrical implementation are presented in the electrical/net-energy section; this subsection is retained only to introduce the physical coupling and must not be read as the current thermal-model status.

\section{Limitations and engineering interpretation}
The analytical work already answers structural questions without claiming a winning geometry. Curvature can increase active surface area within a fixed footprint, but area alone is not energy. \section{Two-technique solution strategy}
The mathematical core is developed through two independent routes so that the numerical geometry can be verified before it is used on shapes without closed-form solutions.

\subsection{Technique 1: analytical route}
For geometries admitting closed-form treatment, the surface remains continuous. The paraboloidal mushroom provides the principal verification target:
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{PV}}
=\frac{\pi R^2}{6k^2}
\left[(1+4k^2)^{3/2}-1\right],
\qquad k=\frac{h}{R}.
\end{equation}
Under the ideal isotropic unobstructed diffuse benchmark,
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{diff}}
=\frac{\eta DHI}{2}
\left(A_{\mathrm{PV}}+A_{\mathrm{foot}}\right).
\end{equation}
These exact expressions are verification targets rather than annual Singapore-yield predictions.

\subsection{Technique 2: discrete surface route}
The same surface is independently approximated by triangular facets. For triangle $i$,
\begin{equation}
A_i=\frac12\|\mathbf e_{i1}\times\mathbf e_{i2}\|,
\qquad
\mathbf n_i=
\frac{\mathbf e_{i1}\times\mathbf e_{i2}}
{\|\mathbf e_{i1}\times\mathbf e_{i2}\|}.
\end{equation}
The discrete area and ideal diffuse benchmark are
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{PV}}^{(N)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_i,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{diff}}^{(N)}
=
\frac{\eta DHI}{2}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_i(1+n_{z,i}).
\end{equation}
Verification requires
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}A_{\mathrm{PV}}^{(N)}
=A_{\mathrm{PV}}^{(\mathrm{analytical})}
}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}P_{\mathrm{diff}}^{(N)}
=P_{\mathrm{diff}}^{(\mathrm{analytical})}.
}
\end{equation}
The canonical Rust binary \texttt{discrete-verification} performs this refinement experiment. After verification, the same facet machinery can be applied to folded, petal, faceted and free-form candidates.

\subsection{Role of FTCS}
FTCS is not used as the primary geometry method because the present problem is a surface-integration/visibility problem rather than a diffusion-type time-dependent PDE. FTCS becomes relevant if a spatially resolved transient module-temperature model is introduced, for example
\begin{equation}
\rho c_p\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
=
k_T\nabla^2T+\dot q.
\end{equation}
Such a thermal model would require its own explicit-scheme stability criterion and spatial/time convergence study; no FTCS thermal result is currently claimed.

\section{Numerical method and reproducibility roadmap}
The numerical implementation is deliberately layered so that each physical addition can be tested before it affects an annual comparison. Geometry is represented in the canonical ENU frame. Analytical shapes provide exact benchmarks; general candidates are discretised into planar facets. For each timestamp the solar-position layer supplies the Sun direction, the irradiance layer supplies compatible direct and diffuse inputs, and the geometry layer supplies facet positions, areas and normals. Visibility and sky-view calculations then determine which radiation reaches each facet before thermal/electrical conversion and time integration.

The present paraboloid verification mesh demonstrates convergence against its analytical area and ideal-diffuse benchmarks. \section{Electrical and net-energy performance: first implemented conversion kernel}
\subsection{Module temperature: implemented NMOT baseline and model-form sensitivity}
\textbf{Beginner view.} Solar panels get hot in the sun, and hotter photovoltaic modules usually convert a little less of the incident solar energy into electricity.

\textbf{Physical view.} Plane-of-array irradiance heats the module while ambient air and ventilation remove heat. Phase II therefore inserts a replaceable thermal model between the frozen irradiance result and the frozen electrical-conversion kernel.

\includesvg[width=0.95\textwidth,inkscapelatex=false]{../figures/thermal_model_bridge}

\textbf{Engineering sensitivity model.} The Faiman steady-state relation remains the wind-sensitive model-form sensitivity
\begin{equation}
T_m=T_a+\frac{G_{\rm POA}}{U_0+U_1 v_w},
\end{equation}
with $T_m$ and $T_a$ in \si{\degreeCelsius}, $G_{\rm POA}$ in \si{\watt\per\square\metre}, $v_w$ in \si{\metre\per\second}, $U_0$ in \si{\watt\per\square\metre\per\kelvin}, and $U_1$ in \si{\watt\second\per\cubic\metre\per\kelvin}. Sandia PVPMC reports Faiman's combined seven-module fit $U_0=25$ and $U_1=6.84$ in those units; these are literature-generic parameters, not Canadian Solar manufacturer coefficients and not project-fitted values.

\textbf{Audit-63 baseline.} The selected Canadian Solar module supplies NMOT $42\pm3\,^{\circ}$C at 800~W~m$^{-2}$, ambient 20~$^{\circ}$C and wind 1~m~s$^{-1}$. Phase II therefore uses the simple module-specific baseline
\begin{equation}
T_m=T_a+(T_{\rm NMOT}-20^{\circ}\mathrm{C})\frac{G_{\rm POA}}{800~\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}}.
\end{equation}
This is a modelling relation anchored to the manufacturer NMOT property, not measured hourly module temperature. The development weather provides hourly NASA POWER T2M and WS10M at 10~m. The NMOT baseline does not consume hourly wind. Direct WS10M plus generic Faiman coefficients is not promoted as a physical baseline because the wind-reference compatibility is unresolved; no geometry-specific airflow coefficients are authorized. Faiman remains a later wind-sensitive model-form sensitivity.

In plain language, sunlight reaches the panel, the panel warms, temperature changes conversion efficiency, the module produces DC electricity, wiring and inverter stages lose some of that electricity, mechanisms may consume auxiliary power, and the remainder is net useful electrical output. The frozen geometry layer supplies an \textbf{IRRADIANCE RESULT}; the electrical kernel produces a separate \textbf{ELECTRICAL CONVERSION RESULT}. No annual geometry-to-electricity comparison is released in this phase.

For the first bounded thermal model,
\begin{equation}
T_m=T_a+(T_{\rm NMOT}-T_{a,\rm NMOT})\frac{G_{\rm POA}}{G_{\rm NMOT}}.
\end{equation}
The temperature-adjusted efficiency and electrical chain are
\begin{align}
\eta_m&=\eta_{\rm ref}\left[1+\gamma_P(T_m-T_{\rm ref})\right],\\
P_{\rm dc,ideal}&=G_{\rm POA}A_m\eta_m,\\
P_{\rm dc}&=P_{\rm dc,ideal}(1-L_{\rm mis})(1-L_{\rm wire,dc}),\\
P_{\rm ac}&=P_{\rm dc}\eta_{\rm inv}(1-L_{\rm sys}),\\
P_{\rm net}&=P_{\rm ac}-P_{\rm aux},\\
E_{\rm net}&=\sum_t P_{\rm net}(t)\Delta t.
\end{align}
Net power is intentionally allowed to become negative when auxiliary demand exceeds generation; gross DC and AC generation are nonnegative. Negative irradiance is rejected rather than silently clamped.

\subsection{Deterministic thermal--electrical coupling}
The Phase-II coupling layer does not introduce a third physical model. It orchestrates the frozen NMOT thermal foundation and frozen electrical equations:
\[
(G_{\rm POA},T_a)\rightarrow T_m\rightarrow\eta_m(T_m)\rightarrow P_{\rm dc}\rightarrow P_{\rm ac}\rightarrow P_{\rm net}\rightarrow E_{\rm net}.
\]
The thermal module owns $T_m$; an additive electrical API accepts externally supplied module temperature so the coupling does not recompute or duplicate thermal physics. Each result retains $T_m$, efficiency, ideal and delivered DC power, AC power, explicit auxiliary power, net power and timestep energy.

At Continue \#191 this is a deterministic validation layer only. The NMOT 39/42/45~$^{\circ}$C sensitivity is propagated through the same electrical design basis; with the selected negative $P_{\max}$ temperature coefficient, the hotter NMOT scenario produces lower electrical power for otherwise identical nonzero inputs. Annual geometry electrical results remain \textbf{NOT AUTHORIZED}. Future annual coupling must join irradiance and ambient temperature by exact canonical absolute UTC timestamp, never row index or nearest-neighbour matching.

\subsection{Electrical design-basis evidence plan}
The provisional rigid-PV module basis is selected and provenance-linked; system-loss and inverter parameters remain unset until separately sourced. The minimum parameter set is:
\begin{center}\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Parameter & Symbol & Unit & Evidence requirement\\
\midrule
STC module efficiency & $\eta_{\rm STC}$ & -- & manufacturer datasheet / certified basis\\
Active module area & $A_m$ & \si{\square\metre} & manufacturer dimensions/active-area basis\\
Power temperature coefficient & $\gamma_P$ & \si{\per\degreeCelsius} & manufacturer datasheet\\
Reference temperature & $T_{\rm ref}$ & \si{\degreeCelsius} & STC definition/datasheet\\
NOCT/NMOT or thermal parameters & -- & \si{\degreeCelsius} & certified datasheet or validated thermal literature\\
DC mismatch/wiring loss & $L_{\rm dc}$ & -- & explicit engineering assumption/source + sensitivity\\
Inverter conversion & $\eta_{\rm inv}$ & -- & selected inverter curve/datasheet or sourced model\\
Other system losses & $L_{\rm sys}$ & -- & explicit component breakdown + sensitivity\\
Auxiliary energy & $E_{\rm aux}$ & \si{\kilo\watt\hour\per\year} & mechanism-specific calculation, zero only for fixed cases\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}\end{center}
Geometry dependence must be declared parameter by parameter: incident irradiance and temperature are geometry-dependent; intrinsic module STC properties are not unless different technologies are intentionally compared. Loss and auxiliary assumptions require uncertainty/sensitivity where they materially affect design comparisons.

A generic implementation chain is
\begin{align}
T_m(t)&=\mathcal{T}\!\left(G_{\rm POA}(t),T_a(t),u(t);\boldsymbol\theta_T\right),\\
\eta_m(t)&=\eta_{\rm STC}\left[1+\gamma_P\left(T_m(t)-T_{\rm ref}\right)\right],\\
P_{\rm dc}(t)&=G_{\rm POA}(t)A_{\rm PV}\eta_m(t)\left(1-L_{\rm dc}\right),\\
P_{\rm ac}(t)&=\mathcal{I}\!\left(P_{\rm dc}(t)\right)\left(1-L_{\rm sys}\right),\\
E_{\rm net,yr}&=\sum_t P_{\rm ac}(t)\Delta t-E_{\rm aux}.
\end{align}
Here $G_{\rm POA}$ is plane-of-array irradiance, $T_a$ ambient temperature, $u$ wind speed or other thermal-model input, $\boldsymbol\theta_T$ the selected thermal-model parameters, $T_m$ module temperature, and $\mathcal{I}$ the selected inverter conversion model. These equations define architecture only until their empirical/design parameters are sourced and validated.

\section{Cost and techno-economics evidence boundary}
No LCOE or project-cost result is released in Phase I. CAPEX, OPEX, lifetime, replacement, mechanism and uncertainty inputs remain to be sourced and modelled.

\section{Carbon and sustainability evidence boundary}
No headline CO2-reduction result is released in Phase I. Later carbon analysis must distinguish operational grid displacement from embodied/lifecycle effects and retain Singapore-specific source provenance.

\section{Integrated design comparison evidence boundary}
The controlled irradiance comparison is one input to the eventual integrated comparison. Electrical net energy, deployability, structural, manufacturing, cost and carbon layers are not yet sufficiently complete for a multi-criterion design conclusion.

\section{Recommended solution concepts evidence boundary}
No universal geometry recommendation is released at Phase-I exit. Later recommendations must be scenario-specific and supported by all relevant upstream evidence rather than by irradiance performance alone.

\section{Limitations and future work}
Phase-I limitations include DEVELOPMENT\_NOT\_SERIS weather/irradiance status, simplified thermal treatment inside the narrow electrical kernel, absent geometry-specific electrical mismatch, absent detailed inverter/MPPT and string/bypass-diode behaviour, unresolved moving-system auxiliary energy, and incomplete structural, manufacturing, cost and carbon models. These are explicit Phase-II-and-later evidence gates, not hidden assumptions.

\section{Conclusion}
Phase I establishes a reproducible and audited foundation for testing whether three-dimensional PV can improve land-normalised solar utilisation in Singapore. The founding mushroom-head concept remains a research origin rather than a presumed winner. The fixed-geometry irradiance comparison is frozen at DEVELOPMENT\_NOT\_SERIS status, deployable single-crease and accordion fixtures are frozen only in narrow kinematic scopes, and the first geometry-agnostic electrical conversion kernel is frozen only as a bounded API/test foundation. Phase II must determine how audited irradiance differences translate into defensible net electrical-energy differences and engineering penalties before stronger design conclusions are permitted.
